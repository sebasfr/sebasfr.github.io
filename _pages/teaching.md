      <div class="teaching-course-body">
        <h3 class="teaching-course-title">Introductory Economics for Non-Majors</h3>
        <p class="teaching-course-subtitle" lang="es"><em>Introducción a la Economía (para estudiantes de otras carreras)</em></p>
        <p class="teaching-course-role mono">LECTURER </p>
        <p class="teaching-course-desc">
          An introductory course designed for non-economics majors. My aim is to help students
          develop a working interest in the national and international economic environment.
          The syllabus introduces the foundational tools of economic theory — supply and demand,
          market structures, and market failures such as externalities and public goods —
          alongside core macroeconomic concepts: production, inflation, employment, fiscal
          and monetary policy, and the dynamics of an open economy. A central thread is
          contextualizing these ideas within the Costa Rican economy, examining local
          challenges around poverty, inequality, and the welfare state, so that students
          across disciplines can take part in informed economic discussions.
        </p>
      </div>
    </li>

    <li class="teaching-course">
      <p class="teaching-course-code mono">EC-1100</p>
      <div class="teaching-course-body">
        <h3 class="teaching-course-title">Introductory Economics</h3>
        <p class="teaching-course-subtitle" lang="es"><em>Introducción a la Economía · B.A. in Economics, first year</em></p>
        <p class="teaching-course-role mono">LECTURER · LABORATORY SESSIONS</p>
        <p class="teaching-course-desc">
          The rigorous foundational course for first-year students in the Economics
          program. It establishes the discipline's terminology, logical reasoning,
          and analytical toolkit, systematically covering the decision-making of
          households, firms, and government — consumer and producer problems, market
          efficiency, and market failures — together with aggregate macroeconomic
          behavior, including GDP, inflation, and aggregate demand and supply. I lead
          the weekly practical laboratory sessions, where students work through the
          mathematical, graphical, and analytical applications of the lecture material
          to build a hands-on framework for interpreting real-world economic problems.
        </p>
      </div>
    </li>
  </ol>
