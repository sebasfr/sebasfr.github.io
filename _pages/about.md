I recently graduated with a B.A. (Honors) in Economics from the University of Costa Rica (UCR). To strengthen my quantitative background, I also completed advanced coursework—including measure theory, functional analysis, and abstract algebra—toward a B.A. in Mathematics. My core research interests lie in **Quantitative Economics, Macroeconomics, and Monetary Economics**. Currently, I work as a Research Assistant under the mentorship of Dr. Jose P. Vasquez and Dr. Isabela Manelici (LSE), primarily contributing on the project *Responsible Sourcing? Evidence from Costa Rica* (Revise and Resubmit at the *American Economic Review*). Through July 2026, I also taught introductory economics courses as a Lecturer at the UCR Department of Economics.

I am now a student in the [**RoME Master in Economics**](https://www.romemaster.it/), a rigorous two-year graduate program jointly run by the **Einaudi Institute for Economics and Finance (EIEF)** and **LUISS Guido Carli University** in Rome. The program is known for its small cohorts, intense tutoring, and a curriculum built around advanced quantitative methods applied to policy-relevant questions.

My research utilizes structural modeling and time-series techniques to analyze inflation dynamics and monetary policy in small open economies. Driven by a desire to push the boundaries of these analytical frameworks, my ultimate goal in quantitative economics is to shift from applied analysis to methodological development, creating the mathematical tools necessary to structurally estimate novel and increasingly complex macroeconomic models.

Recently, I was awarded first place in the 2024 Latin American Reserve Fund (FLAR) Research Contest. My winning paper, *"Supply Shocks, Monetary Policy and Foreign Investment: The Costa Rican Case"*, investigates Costa Rica's atypical experience of accelerated growth and deflation following global supply shocks. Using a New Keynesian Growth Model, I demonstrate that the interplay between foreign investment attraction and nearshoring was a key driver of this outcome.
