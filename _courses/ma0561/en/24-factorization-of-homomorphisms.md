## The factorization theorem

### Theorem (Factorization of a homomorphism through a quotient)

Let $$R, S$$ be rings and $$I$$ an $$R$$-ideal. Let $$f : R \to S$$ be a homomorphism such that $$I \subseteq \operatorname{Ker}(f)$$. Then there exists a unique homomorphism $$\bar{f} : R/I \to S$$ such that

$$
\bar{f}([x]) = f(x) \quad \text{for all } x \in R.
$$

Equivalently, $$\bar{f} \circ \pi_{I} = f$$, that is, the following diagram commutes:

![The factorization of a homomorphism through the quotient](/assets/img/courses/ma0561/factorizacion-de-homomorfismos.svg)

***Proof:*** *$$\bar{f}$$ is well defined.* If $$[x] = [y]$$, then

$$
[x] = [y] \iff x - y \in I \subseteq \operatorname{Ker}(f) \implies f(x - y) = f(x) - f(y) = 0_{S} \implies f(x) = f(y),
$$

and therefore $$\bar{f}([x]) = \bar{f}([y])$$.

*$$\bar{f}$$ is a homomorphism.* As in the first isomorphism theorem, this is inherited directly from $$f$$:

$$
\bar{f}([x] + [y]) = \bar{f}([x + y]) = f(x + y) = f(x) + f(y) = \bar{f}([x]) + \bar{f}([y]),
$$

and analogously for the product; moreover $$\bar{f}([1]) = f(1) = 1_{S}$$.

*Uniqueness.* If $$g : R/I \to S$$ satisfies $$g([x]) = f(x)$$ for all $$x \in R$$, then $$g$$ and $$\bar{f}$$ agree on all the classes $$[x]$$, which are all the elements of $$R/I$$; hence $$g = \bar{f}$$.

### Theorem (Bijection between homomorphisms vanishing on $$I$$ and homomorphisms from the quotient)

Let $$R, S$$ be rings and $$I$$ an $$R$$-ideal. The map $$f \mapsto \bar{f}$$ is a bijection

$$
\{ f \in \operatorname{Hom}(R, S) :\ f(I) = \{0\} \} \longrightarrow \operatorname{Hom}(R/I, S).
$$

***Proof:*** Exercise.

### Exercise (The unique homomorphism from $$\mathbb{Z}$$)

If $$f : \mathbb{Z} \to R$$, with $$R$$ a ring, is a homomorphism, then $$f(n) = n \cdot 1_{R}$$; in particular, there is a unique homomorphism $$\mathbb{Z} \to R$$.

### Example (The homomorphisms from $$\mathbb{Z}_{m}$$ to $$\mathbb{Z}_{n}$$)

Let $$m, n \in \mathbb{Z}^{+}$$. What are the elements of $$\operatorname{Hom}(\mathbb{Z}_{m}, \mathbb{Z}_{n})$$? By the bijection of the previous theorem, with $$R = \mathbb{Z}$$ and $$I = (m)$$,

$$
|\operatorname{Hom}(\mathbb{Z}/(m), \mathbb{Z}_{n})| = \Big| \underbrace{\{ f : \mathbb{Z} \to \mathbb{Z}_{n}\ \text{homomorphism} :\ f((m)) = \{[0]_{n}\} \}}_{A} \Big|.
$$

Using the previous exercise, if $$f \in A$$, then $$f(x) = x \cdot [1]_{n} = [x]_{n}$$; that is, the only candidate is reduction modulo $$n$$. This candidate belongs to $$A$$ if and only if $$f((m)) = \{[0]_{n}\}$$, and since $$(m)$$ is generated by $$m$$, this is equivalent to $$f(m) = [0]_{n}$$. Note that if $$f(m) \neq [0]_{n}$$, then $$A = \emptyset$$ and $$|\operatorname{Hom}(\mathbb{Z}_{m}, \mathbb{Z}_{n})| = 0$$. On the other hand,

$$
f(m) = [0]_{n} \iff m \cdot [1]_{n} = [0]_{n} \iff [m]_{n} = [0]_{n} \iff n \mid m.
$$

In that case $$|A| = 1$$, so that $$|\operatorname{Hom}(\mathbb{Z}_{m}, \mathbb{Z}_{n})| = 1$$, and the unique homomorphism is

$$
x + (m) \mapsto x + (n), \qquad \text{that is,} \qquad [x]_{m} \mapsto [x]_{n}.
$$

### Theorem (Extension of homomorphisms to the polynomial ring)

Let $$A, B$$ be commutative rings, $$f \in \operatorname{Hom}(A, B)$$ and $$b \in B$$. Then there exists a unique $$\bar{f} \in \operatorname{Hom}(A[x], B)$$ such that

$$
\bar{f}\left( \sum_{i=0}^{n} a_{i} x^{i} \right) = \sum_{i=0}^{n} f(a_{i})\, b^{i}.
$$

## Quotients by prime and maximal ideals

### Theorem (Prime is equivalent to the quotient being a domain; maximal is equivalent to the quotient being a field)

Let $$R$$ be a commutative ring and $$I$$ an $$R$$-ideal. Then:

1. $$I$$ is prime $$\iff$$ $$R/I$$ is an integral domain;
2. $$I$$ is maximal $$\iff$$ $$R/I$$ is a field.

***Proof:*** *(a), “$$\Leftarrow$$”.* Suppose that $$R/I$$ is an integral domain, and let $$a, b \in R$$ be such that $$a b \in I$$. Then

$$
(a + I)(b + I) = a b + I = I,
$$

that is, the product of the classes is the zero of $$R/I$$. Since $$R/I$$ is an integral domain, it has no zero divisors, and so one of the two factors must be zero: $$a + I = I$$ or $$b + I = I$$. That is, $$a \in I$$ or $$b \in I$$, whence we conclude that $$I$$ is prime.

*(a), “$$\Rightarrow$$”.* Suppose that $$I$$ is prime. Note first that $$R/I$$ is commutative, since $$R$$ is. Let $$a + I, b + I \in R/I$$ be such that

$$
(a + I)(b + I) = a b + I = I,
$$

that is, $$a b \in I$$. By the primality of $$I$$, we have that $$a \in I$$ or $$b \in I$$; supposing without loss of generality that $$a \in I$$, we obtain $$a + I = I$$. That is, if a product of classes is zero, then one of the factors is zero, so that $$R/I$$ has no zero divisors and is an integral domain.

*(b), “$$\Rightarrow$$”.* Suppose that $$I$$ is maximal. Since $$I \neq R$$, we have that $$1 \notin I$$, and so $$[1] \neq [0]$$ in $$R/I$$. Let $$x \in R$$ be such that $$[x] \neq [0]$$. By the correspondence theorem, the ideals of $$R/I$$ correspond to the ideals of $$R$$ that contain $$I$$; by the maximality of $$I$$, these are only $$I$$ and $$R$$, so the only $$R/I$$-ideals are $$I/I = \{[0]\}$$ and $$R/I$$. Consider the ideal $$\langle [x] \rangle$$: since $$[x] \neq [0]$$, we have that $$\langle [x] \rangle \neq \{[0]\}$$, and so $$\langle [x] \rangle = R/I$$. Since $$[1] \in R/I = \langle [x] \rangle = \{ [y][x] :\ [y] \in R/I \}$$, there exists $$[y] \in R/I$$ such that $$[y][x] = [1]$$, that is, $$[x]$$ is a unit. We conclude that every non-zero element of $$R/I$$ is invertible and, together with commutativity and $$[1] \neq [0]$$, that $$R/I$$ is a field.

*(b), “$$\Leftarrow$$”.* Suppose that $$R/I$$ is a field. Then $$[1] \neq [0]$$ in $$R/I$$, that is, $$1 \notin I$$, so that $$I \neq R$$. Now let $$J$$ be an ideal such that $$I \subseteq J$$ and $$J \neq I$$; we must show that $$J = R$$. By the correspondence theorem, $$J/I$$ is an ideal of $$R/I$$, and since there exists $$x \in J \setminus I$$, we have that $$[x] \in J/I$$ with $$[x] \neq [0]$$, that is, $$J/I \neq \{[0]\}$$. But a field has only the two trivial ideals, so $$J/I = R/I$$. Since the correspondence $$J \mapsto J/I$$ is a bijection between the ideals of $$R$$ that contain $$I$$ and the ideals of $$R/I$$, from $$J/I = R/I$$ we conclude that $$J = R$$. That is, $$I$$ is maximal.
