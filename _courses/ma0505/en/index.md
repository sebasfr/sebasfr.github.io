The first course of the analysis sequence at UCR, taught as *Análisis I*, following
Dr. Pedro Méndez Hernández's lectures. It falls into two halves. The first builds
analysis on a general metric space: norms on $$\mathbb{R}^{d}$$, open and closed
sets, interior and closure, continuity and its topological characterisations,
compactness in both its sequential and open-cover forms, completeness and the
completion of a metric space, the Arzelà–Ascoli theorem, connectedness and
path-connectedness, and the Baire category theorems.

The second half develops measure theory: functions of bounded variation and the
Jordan decomposition, the Riemann–Stieltjes integral, Lebesgue outer measure and
measurability, the σ-algebra of Lebesgue measurable sets, the Cantor set and a
non-measurable set, measurable functions, the theorems of Egorov and Lusin,
convergence in measure, the Lebesgue integral first for non-negative functions and
then in general, the monotone and dominated convergence theorems, and finally
Lebesgue's characterisation of Riemann integrability.

These are personal notes I took as a student. They were written in Spanish and
translated into English; both versions are available and you can switch between
them at any point. They are shared as-is, may contain errors, and are not
affiliated with the instructor.
